# Research Mainline Document

## Recommended V1/V2 Structure

1. Version, date, evidence scope, and document status.
2. Citation system and content-ownership convention.
3. Project-facing conclusion.
4. One or two main-method papers per principal branch.
5. Venue tier, selection rationale, and comparison with alternatives.
6. Project method definition or route.
7. Required inputs, data outputs, and tool responsibilities.
8. Validation ladder.
9. Execution checklist.
10. Applicability and prohibited claims.
11. References.

## Main-Method Introduction

At the first concentrated discussion of each main paper, explain:

- research object and problem;
- method/model/experiment chain;
- key results and validation;
- contribution to the project’s input, step, bridge variable, validation, or boundary;
- components that can be transferred;
- conditions or conclusions that cannot be transferred;
- why this paper was selected over alternatives;
- venue context and its verification basis when relevant to selection or policy.

Use nearby academic citations for literature facts.

## High-Weight Reader Asset

Require:

- original PDF;
- canonical source card;
- detailed Chinese or Chinese–English aligned full-paper reader;
- PDF page numbers or stable block anchors;
- figure/table locations;
- terminology and uncertainty notes;
- author contribution and project transfer in separate sections;
- applicability and non-transferable boundaries.

Do not substitute a short Chinese summary for the reader.

## Ownership Language

Use:

- “作者等报告/建立/测得/指出……” for literature facts;
- “本项目据此采用/定义/扩展……” for project design;
- “待验证：……” for hypotheses.

Do not write a software import capability as proof of physical solving capability. Do not write qualitative trend or topology agreement as quantitative accuracy.

## Link Closure

Use clickable relative Markdown links:

- mainline → reader, source card, PDF;
- reader → source card, PDF;
- source card → reader, PDF;
- manifest → all three assets.

Code-formatted paths alone do not count as links.

## Completion Gate

Do not mark the mainline ready when:

- a main paper fails the task contract's relevance, method, validation, source-quality, or configured venue requirements;
- full text or page anchors are missing;
- reader/source/PDF links are broken;
- selection rationale or non-transferable boundaries are absent;
- citations and ownership are ambiguous;
- a display equation lacks complete symbol definitions.
